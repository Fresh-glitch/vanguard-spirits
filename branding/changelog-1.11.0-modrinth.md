# 1.11.0 — Set down, and lit

The Binding Altar stops being a machine you feed and becomes a place you leave things. And several things now hold their light in the dark.

## Added

- **The altar keeps what you put on it.** Close the screen and the charm and Fractured Memories stay where you left them — through a reload, a trip to the surface, a night's sleep. Break the altar and everything on it drops.
- **And it shows them.** The charm lies flat in the rune ring cut into the table, and the memories you have paid toward its binding turn slowly above it. Past eight they fill a second ring, higher and turning the other way, so a full stack of sixteen is exactly two full rings — you can read how close a binding is from across the room without opening anything.
- **All of it while the screen is open, too.** Lay a charm in the slot and it appears on the stone in the same moment; take a binding and the orbit thins as the memories are spent.
- **Things glow in the dark.** A charm's rune burns while the deepslate plate around it stays unlit. The Fractured Memory's crack burns — the crack only, not the stone it runs through — and cycles through the four charm colours as it always has. The **Echo of Kinship** burns whole. All of it applies wherever the item is: in your hand, dropped on the ground, in an item frame, or lying on an altar.

## Changed

- The memories circling an altar light themselves, and rise and fall in brightness with the colour they are currently showing — dimmest on the Wanderer's green, brightest on the Leaper's blue.

## Notes

- **The Binding Altar can no longer be pushed by pistons.** Minecraft refuses to push any block that stores contents, and the altar stores them now. If you have one in a piston contraption, it will stop moving.
- Altars already standing in your world pick all of this up; nothing needs rebuilding and no existing charm or ruin is affected.
- While one player has an altar open, another opening the same one finds it empty — the first player is holding its contents until they close the screen. This is deliberate: it is what stops two people carrying off the same charm.

## Requires
